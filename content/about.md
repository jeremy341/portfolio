# About Jeremy Darko

Jeremy Darko | Computer Engineering & ML Systems

## Short profile

I'm Jeremy. I'm a student from NRW, Germany, and I have a slight problem with leaving things alone once I start wondering how they work.

## Biography

When I was younger, I used to take the spare pieces from my LEGO sets and mix them together because following the instructions got boring. Years later, apparently not much has changed. The pieces are just more expensive now.

My route into computers was pretty random. I started programming, got annoyed because my PC was slow, and decided the reasonable solution was to learn how the entire thing worked. Then I started configuring PCs for friends, built a few of them, saved up €400 and eventually convinced my dad to let me build my own from used parts.

I'm still using that PC, by the way.

That was probably where software stopped being enough for me.

These days I'm most interested in what happens underneath it. Why can my AMD GPU draw millions of pixels but PyTorch doesn't want to train on it? Why can a model score 90.6% in validation and still make me nervous when I point a camera at real waste? Why does clicking a button labelled "Start pump" turn into a serial-protocol problem?

Those questions became [TorchVK](/work/torchvk/), [MIRA](/work/mira/) and [FluidicStudio](/work/fluidicstudio/).

That last one actually started with me asking about an internship at Fraunhofer IMS. It didn't work out, but I ended up spending four weeks working with people at the University of Duisburg-Essen instead. I knew next to nothing about microfluidics when I said yes.

Which, looking back, probably made the whole thing a lot more interesting.

I also spend a lot of time around [Hack Club](https://hackclub.com/), which is partly responsible for getting me back into building when programming had started becoming boring. It gave me an excuse to make an ESP32 alarm clock, [Poorup](/work/poorup/) and several other things that became significantly larger than I originally intended.

I'm still figuring out exactly where all of this leads. Computer engineering is probably the closest label: ML systems, GPU computing, embedded hardware and whatever lives between those things.

I don't really care about knowing one technology just for the sake of putting it on a list. I like reaching the point in a project where something stops making sense, because that's usually where I end up learning the most.

I'm also actively looking for opportunities to work with other people. So if you're building something interesting, working on a project, or just think we could make something cool together, please [reach out](/contact/). I'm especially looking for an internship during my Easter school holidays in spring 2027, preferably involving computer engineering, machine learning or hardware. Though I'm always open to something outside those areas too.

And despite what the rest of this page might suggest, I occasionally do things that don't involve a computer.

Occasionally.

I keep short updates on [what I’m working on now](/now/).
